# Responsibility and Coordination in a Family-Owned SME

*An ANT and systems-theoretical case study of diffuse responsibility — Work in Progress*

## Overview

This project examines how responsibility is produced, distributed and diffused within a family-owned SME through interactions between people, digital systems, documents, routines and organisational communication.

Rather than treating responsibility as an individual attribute, the analysis approaches the organisation as a sociomaterial network in which decisions emerge through interactions between human and non-human actors.

The study combines:

- Actor-Network Theory (ANT)
- Luhmann’s systems theory, particularly decision communication and decision premises

The case is anonymised and presented at an analytical level to foreground organisational mechanisms rather than identifiable actors or sites.

## Empirical Vignette

The central office functions as an administrative hub connecting multiple parts of the organisation. Its material environment reflects the accumulation of tasks and organisational pressures: faded yellow walls and old storage armoires surround functional desks on a worn wooden floor. New PCs and laptops coexist with piles of documents and office supplies, while exposed wiring coils around the workstations.

This juxtaposition of older material arrangements with newer digital technologies is analytically relevant rather than merely aesthetic. Shared spreadsheets, email inboxes, paper documentation and informal communication coexist. Tasks can therefore become highly visible without responsibility for them becoming equally clear.

The office is approached here not simply as a physical setting, but as a sociomaterial coordination point through which organisational uncertainty is translated into administrative action.

## Core Insight

Responsibility is not simply assigned or avoided. It can also be structurally produced — and diffused — through organisational arrangements such as:

- shared communication channels
- informal task distribution (“everyone does everything”)
- spreadsheets and documents without clear ownership
- overlapping digital and paper-based workflows
- routinised decision patterns

These elements act as mediators that shape how decisions are made, how uncertainty is absorbed and how accountability becomes distributed across the organisation.

## Analytical Focus

The analysis focuses on:

- how organisational irritations are translated into decision situations
- how decision premises absorb uncertainty
- how sociomaterial arrangements stabilise routines
- how digital tools and documents participate in coordination
- how responsibility becomes diffused across the network

## Key Concepts

- Decision communication
- Decision premises
- Uncertainty absorption
- Deparadoxisation
- Actor-networks
- Mediators
- Obligatory passage points

## Research Material

The study draws on:

- four semi-structured interviews
- informal conversations and observational material
- organisational artefacts and internal documentation
- digital systems, spreadsheets and administrative routines

All identifying details are removed or generalised.

## Status

This is an ongoing exploratory qualitative case study.

The analysis remains provisional and may change as the empirical interpretation develops. The project is not presented as a completed publication.
